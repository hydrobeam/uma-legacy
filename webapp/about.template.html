How this works

The planner searches four-uma loops, outfits, race schedules and support decks from what you own, and ranks them by the chance that one career ends with every target skill as a spark on the veteran. Independent Training only.

How the trainee acquires a skill

  • Scenario. Unity Cup gives every trainee the No Stopping Me! and It's On! golds.
  • Outfit. Skills on the trainee's own skill list: Agnes Digital [Full-Color Fangirling] has Superstan; Tamamo Cross [Fast as Lightning] and Inari One [Edomurasaki] have Tail Nine. Yamanin Zephyr gets Slipstream from a goal race.
  • Support deck. Training hints and card events.
  • Inheritance. Two inspirations per career from the parents and grandparents.

Assumptions

  1. 8/8 legacies: both parents are 8/8, and so are all four grandparents: each carries every target spark, as in a running loop. A loop still being built has fewer carriers and lower odds.
  2. Spark generation: P(spark) = b × 1.1n, with b = 20% for a white or 40% for a gold, and n the number of the six ancestors already carrying that spark. In a running loop n = 6: 35.4% and 70.9%.
  3. Star mix of legacy sparks: 50/45/5% for 1/2/3 stars below SS; 20/70/10% at SS.
  4. Every card at max limit break.
  5. IT's event choices: IT takes the top choice unless a prioritised skill sits only on another choice, and priorities are set to reach the hints counted.
  6. Pal events: pal finales always fire; pal first-year date events don't fire in IT.

Limitations

  1. The exact choices IT takes at events are still unclear.
  2. The counted hints rely on the player's IT priorities; the deck card names the one the planner knows (Sasami Anshinzawa SSR: prioritise Risky Business).
  3. Not modelled: random character events from support cards (about one career in six; under 2% on the odds); conditional and secret events, except Tokai Teio's Head-On; hint levels and skill-point costs; Grand Concert's low rate for the first choice of Sasami Anshinzawa SSR's finale.
  4. Placeholders: Grand Masters' hint base (2.30) is a guess, with no IT runs to set it. R cards are off by default: their hint and event model is untested. The Grand Masters-era data is the JP game data cut at L'Arc's launch.
  5. Data: card events come from cached GameTora files and the game data from one Global master.mdb snapshot.
  6. Every measured rate comes from uma-it uploads.

The loop

Four umas take turns as the trainee. Her parents are two of the other three; her grandparents are their parents from their own last careers. No uma is her own ancestor. In a running loop all six ancestors carry every target spark. The odds shown are the average over the 12 trainee and parent arrangements.

Affinity and races

Affinity raises how often a legacy passes its sparks (see Spark inheritance odds). It is the umas' fixed relation points (pairs and trios) plus 3 for each G1 both won. The race schedules are chosen to maximise the loop's shared G1 wins; the Affinity button shows the totals.

Example: Oguri Cap and Agnes Digital have 30 relation points, and 21 shared G1s add 63.

In a schedule, "A or B" means either race at the same odds; all four umas must run the same one. Optional G1s add race sparks, not affinity.

The deck

  • Training hints: the game doesn't publish how often a card hints. These rates are inferred from what real shared IT runs show, not read from the game's data, so they are estimates. The rate scales with the card's hint frequency: base × (1 + hint frequency / 100) hints per career, each a random pick among the card's skills still below hint level 5. The base differs by scenario: Unity Cup 2.70, Grand Concert 2.03, Trackblazer 1.81, URA Finale 1.60 (Grand Masters 2.30, a placeholder). A card with 30% hint frequency hints about 3.5 times in Unity Cup; with five hintable skills, a given one comes up in about half of careers.
  • Chain events: the number of story-chain events in a career is random, also estimated from real runs: 7.35 on average, between 5 and 10 in 83% of careers. Each one advances a non-pal card's chain, picked at random, a card with more links left more often; the same in every scenario. Three 3-link cards (9 links) usually leave some last links unplayed. Pals have their own storyline and take none.
  • Event outcomes: 50/50 unless measured. Measured at 75%: Sasami Anshinzawa SSR's Nothing Ventured, Riko Kashimoto SSR's Ramp Up, Seiun Sky [Foolproof Plan]'s Fast-Paced, Smart Falcon [My Umadol Way! ☆]'s Center Stage.
  • A card marked "prioritise X" needs X set as an IT priority for its event to give the counted reward.
  • One friend's card can be borrowed. By default each uma gets her own best deck; a result shows the shared deck, then each uma's swaps.
  • No trainee can use a card of herself or a group card she belongs to. Team Sirius is banned in Unity Cup and Grand Concert.

Reading a result

  • 1 in 119 runs to land all 8: one career in 119 on average. "7 of 8 or better" counts careers that miss at most one.
  • 90% chance within 273 runs: the number of careers that gives a 90% chance of at least one success. After 119 careers, 37% of players still have none.
  • As trainee: the odds when that uma is the trainee; they differ by outfit.

Spark inheritance odds

A career has two inspirations, in April of the second and third years. At each one, every legacy (two parents and four grandparents) rolls each of its sparks separately:

P(pass) = base × (1 + affinity / 100)

  • Base by spark type and stars (1/2/3★): skill (white) 3/6/9%, race 1/2/3%, unique (green) 5/10/15%, aptitude (pink) 1/3/5%, stat (blue) 70/80/90%.
  • Stars of a target spark: 50/45/5% below SS, an average base of 4.65%; 20/70/10% at SS, 5.7%.
  • Affinity is that legacy's own, so parents pass sparks far more often than grandparents.

A target is inherited when any of the twelve rolls (six legacies, two inspirations) passes: P = 1 − Π (1 − P(pass)).

Example: a parent at affinity 286 passes a given skill spark about 18% of the time per inspiration, a grandparent at 87 about 9%. A skill all six legacies carry is inherited in about 78% of careers.

The model, in detail

Model. The odds of one career, for a trainee T with parents P1 and P2 and their four parents:

  • Inheritance. A target is inherited with P = 1 − Π (1 − b × (1 + affinity / 100))² over the six legacies, two inspirations each; b = 4.65% below SS, 5.7% at SS (the star mix averaged over 3/6/9%).
  • Affinity per legacy. Parent P: pair(T, P) + pair(P1, P2) + trio(T, P, G) for each of P's own parents G, + 3 per G1 P shares with the other parent and with each G. Grandparent G under P: trio(T, P, G) + 3 per G1 G shares with P. pair and trio are the game's relation points; in a loop P's own parents are the other parent and the fourth uma, so the parents' shared G1s count in both terms.
  • Acquiring a target. Guaranteed by the scenario, on the trainee's own outfit skill list, hinted by the deck, or inherited. Each source gives the gold or the white; the target's gold chance and white-only chance are kept apart.
  • Landing. P(target) = P(gold) × 0.709 + P(white, no gold) × 0.354. The career lands all targets with the product over targets, corrected for what targets share (below).
  • The loop's odds are the average over its 12 careers (4 trainees × 3 parent pairs); each uma heads 3 of them.

Deck. Six cards, every card at max limit break; group cards count as pals.

  • Training hints. h = base × (1 + hint frequency / 100) draws per career (the floor or ceiling of h, weighted by its fraction). Each draw picks uniformly among the card's skills still below hint level 5; a skill leaves the pool once it reaches level 5, so a card giving +5 levels per hint never repeats one. One card's draws over several targets are computed jointly: a card can't hint every target in its pool at once. Base: Unity Cup 2.70, Grand Concert 2.03, Trackblazer 1.81, URA Finale 1.60, Grand Masters 2.30 (placeholder).
  • Chain events. Binomial(21, 0.35) per career (mean 7.35). Each advances one remaining chain link of a non-pal card, chosen with weight equal to the links that card has left; a card's links fire in order. Pals and R cards take no chain events. A skill offered at several links of one card counts once, from the earliest link that fires.
  • Chain ends. A choice that ends a card's chain is offered as a separate deck entry: the card's earlier links, that choice's rewards, and its remaining links freed for the other cards. If IT's default choice ends the chain on some outcomes, the chain stops there at that share, as an exact mixture over where it stops.
  • Event outcomes. One outcome per event, so outcomes exclude each other; 50/50 unless measured (Sasami Anshinzawa SSR's finale, Riko Kashimoto SSR's Ramp Up, Seiun Sky [Foolproof Plan]'s Fast-Paced, Smart Falcon [My Umadol Way! ☆]'s Center Stage: 75%). Where an event offers targets on different choices, the card is offered once per choice worth taking (a choice no other beats on every target) and marked "prioritise X".
  • Pals. Pal finales always fire; first-year date events are left out. A pal entry names the event and choice it relies on, and the skill to prioritise follows from IT's rule: a skill on that choice and on no choice above it.
  • Rules. No trainee uses a card of herself or a group card she belongs to; scenario bans apply; one card may be borrowed.

Race schedules. Each uma's schedule is chosen to maximise the loop's expected shared G1 wins, every pair weighted alike.

  • Win chance of one attempt: 110 − distance cost − surface cost (A 0, B 10, C 20, D 30, E 40, F 60, G 90) − streak penalty (0/0/5/20/30/50 for the 1st to 6th+ race in a row, any race counts, a rest resets it), clamped to 0–100%. A race run in both Classic and Senior counts once: P(win) = 1 − (1 − p₁)(1 − p₂). Two umas' wins are independent, so their expected shared wins are Σ Pi(race) × Pj(race).
  • One uma's best schedule against the other three is solved exactly by dynamic programming: Junior turns in order, then each Classic turn together with the Senior turn 24 later, tracking both streaks and which career goals are met.
  • The four schedules are improved in turns (each uma re-solved against the others until none improves), then by group moves (forcing two or more umas onto the same race together, which no single uma would choose alone), then by 30 rounds of randomised restarts. A last pass adds every optional G1 that costs no shared wins.

Search.

  • Decks are compared per target on (P(gold hint), P(any hint)), which the odds rise with. A deck that another beats or matches on every target is dropped; a card that another legal card beats on every target never enters those decks. With a deck per uma, each uma takes the best legal deck for her open targets from the decks no other deck beats.
  • Loops. Every foursome of owned umas and outfits gets a guaranteed ceiling: exact pairwise bounds on shared G1 wins (each pair's best case with the partner at her best odds) combined with each trainee's best deck. Foursomes are solved best ceiling first, and the search stops when no remaining ceiling can beat the last result shown, so nothing unsearched can beat it (given the schedules the optimiser finds, which are not proven optimal).
  • Exact scoring. The search ranks by the product of per-target odds. That treats targets as independent, but chain cards share one career's chain events, so their rewards land or miss together, and one event's outcomes can give one target or another. The results shown, and every candidate whose ranking value is at least 80% of the best's, are rescored exactly over the distribution of chain progress; the odds shown are these exact values. They ran 0–8% above the product on the reference setups, up to 19% for decks with 0–1 pals.
  • Character rule. With one shared deck, the search first ignores who can use which card; when an answer breaks the rule it splits into loops without that uma and loops whose decks avoid the blocking cards, and searches both.
  • Typical time: under a second with Unity Cup's golds given, 3–11 s without; up to about 100 s for three results. The browser is about 1.5× slower.
