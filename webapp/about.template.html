How this works

The planner searches four-uma loops, outfits, race schedules and support decks from what you own, and ranks them by the chance that one career ends with every target skill as a spark on the veteran. Independent Training only.

How the trainee acquires a skill

  • Scenario. E.g. Unity Cup gives every trainee the No Stopping Me! and It's On! golds.
  • Outfit. Skills on the trainee's own skill list, e.g. Agnes Digital [Full-Color Fangirling] has Superstan.
  • Support deck. Training hints and card events.
  • Inheritance. Two inspirations per career from the parents and grandparents.

Assumptions

  1. 8/8 legacies: both parents are 8/8, and so are all four grandparents: each carries every target spark, as in a running loop. A loop still being built has fewer carriers and lower odds.
  2. Spark generation: P(spark)=b×1.1n, with b = 20% for a white or 40% for a gold, and n the number of the six ancestors already carrying that spark. In a running loop n = 6: 35.4% and 70.9%.
  3. Assumed lineage rank: legacy sparks have 50/45/5% for 1/2/3 stars below SS, 20/70/10% at SS. The rank is chosen in the settings; the planner doesn't work out the score a deck would reach.
  4. Every card at max limit break.
  5. IT's event choices: IT takes the top choice unless a prioritised skill sits only on another choice, and priorities are set to reach the hints counted.
  6. Pal events: pal finales always fire; pal first-year date events don't fire in IT.

Limitations

  1. Only skills are scored: the planner doesn't calculate a career's rank, score or skill points.
  2. The exact choices IT takes at events are still unclear.
  3. The counted hints rely on the player's IT priorities; the deck card names the one the planner knows (Sasami Anshinzawa SSR: prioritise Risky Business).
  4. Not modelled: random character events from support cards (about one career in six; under 2% on the odds); conditional and secret events, except Tokai Teio's Head-On; hint levels and skill-point costs; Grand Concert's low rate for the first choice of Sasami Anshinzawa SSR's finale.
  5. Placeholders: Grand Masters' hint base (2.30) is a guess, with no IT runs to set it. R cards are off by default: their hint and event model is untested. The Grand Masters-era data is the JP game data cut at L'Arc's launch.
  6. Data: card events come from cached GameTora files and the game data from one Global master.mdb snapshot.
  7. Every measured rate comes from uma-it uploads.

The loop

Four umas take turns as the trainee. Her parents are two of the other three; her grandparents are their parents from their own last careers. No uma is her own ancestor. In a running loop all six ancestors carry every target spark. The odds shown are the average over the 12 trainee and parent arrangements.

Affinity and races

Affinity raises how often a legacy passes its sparks (see Spark inheritance odds). It is the umas' fixed relation points (pairs and trios) plus 3 for each G1 both won. The race schedules are chosen to maximise the loop's shared G1 wins; the Affinity button shows the totals.

Example: Oguri Cap and Agnes Digital have 30 relation points, and 21 shared G1s add 63.

In a schedule, "A or B" means either race at the same odds; all four umas must run the same one. Optional G1s add race sparks, not affinity.

The deck

  • Training hints: the game doesn't publish how often a card hints. These rates are inferred from what real shared IT runs show, not read from the game's data, so they are estimates. The rate scales with the card's hint frequency: base×(1+hint frequency/100) hints per career, each a random pick among the card's skills still below hint level 5. The base differs by scenario: Unity Cup 2.70, Grand Concert 2.03, Trackblazer 1.81, URA Finale 1.60 (Grand Masters 2.30, a placeholder). A card with 30% hint frequency hints about 3.5 times in Unity Cup; with five hintable skills, a given one comes up in about half of careers.
  • Chain events: the number of story-chain events in a career is random, also estimated from real runs: 7.35 on average, between 5 and 10 in 83% of careers. Each one advances a non-pal card's chain, picked at random, a card with more links left more often; the same in every scenario. Three 3-link cards (9 links) usually leave some last links unplayed. Pals have their own storyline and take none.
  • Event outcomes: 50/50 unless measured. Measured at 75%: Sasami Anshinzawa SSR's Nothing Ventured, Riko Kashimoto SSR's Ramp Up, Seiun Sky [Foolproof Plan]'s Fast-Paced, Smart Falcon [My Umadol Way! ☆]'s Center Stage.
  • A card marked "prioritise X" needs X set as an IT priority for its event to give the counted reward.
  • One friend's card can be borrowed. By default each uma gets her own best deck; a result shows the shared deck, then each uma's swaps.
  • No trainee can use a card of herself or a group card she belongs to. Team Sirius is banned in Unity Cup and Grand Concert.

Reading a result

  • 1 in 119 runs to land all 8: one career in 119 on average. "7 of 8 or better" counts careers that miss at most one.
  • 90% chance within 273 runs: the number of careers that gives a 90% chance of at least one success. After 119 careers, 37% of players still have none.
  • As trainee: the odds when that uma is the trainee; they differ by outfit.

Spark inheritance odds

A career has two inspirations, in April of the second and third years. At each one, every legacy (two parents and four grandparents) rolls each of its sparks separately:

P(pass)=base×(1+affinity100)
  • Base by spark type and stars (1/2/3★): skill (white) 3/6/9%, race 1/2/3%, unique (green) 5/10/15%, aptitude (pink) 1/3/5%, stat (blue) 70/80/90%.
  • Stars of a target spark: 50/45/5% below SS, an average base of 4.65%; 20/70/10% at SS, 5.7%.
  • Affinity is that legacy's own, so parents pass sparks far more often than grandparents.

A target is inherited when any of the twelve rolls (six legacies, two inspirations) passes, with Pi each roll's P(pass):

P=1−∏i=112(1−Pi)

Example: a parent at affinity 286 passes a given skill spark about 18% of the time per inspiration, a grandparent at 87 about 9%. A skill all six legacies carry is inherited in about 78% of careers.

The model, in detail

Very technical: the full model and search, written so another person or program could reimplement the planner. Nothing here is needed to use it.

Show the model

Symbols: T the trainee, P a parent and P′ the other parent, X the fourth uma of the loop; k a target, D a deck.

Model. 1. Careers. A loop of four umas has 12 careers: a trainee T (4 ways) and the uma X who sits out (3 ways). The other two are the parents; each parent P's own parents are P′ and X (the grandparents). Each uma heads 3 careers.

2. Affinity of each legacy. pair and trio are the game's relation points (the sum over the relation groups all of them belong to); w(i,j) is the expected number of G1s both i and j win, from their schedules (step 9):

AP=pair(T,P)+pair(P,P′)+trio(T,P,P′)+trio(T,P,X)+3[2w(P,P′)+w(P,X)]AG=trio(T,P,G)+3w(P,G), G ∈ {P′, X}

3. Inheritance chance. Every target spark sits on all six legacies (two parents, four grandparents), with stars drawn from the rank's mix s1/s2/s3 (50/45/5% below SS, 20/70/10% at SS). The chance that one roll passes a spark averages the 3/6/9% bases over that mix; two inspirations, so the chance that a target is inherited at least once in the career is the same q for every target:

b=0.03s1+0.06s2+0.09s3q=1−(∏ℓ(1−min(1,b(1+Aℓ100))))2

4. One target's spark chance. For target k: gk the deck's chance of a gold hint, wk its chance of a white hint given no gold hint (step 6), ek the chance the trainee's own events hint it. A skill the trainee learns becomes a spark 0.709 of the time as a gold and 0.354 as a white (0.2×1.16 or 0.4×1.16):

sk=0.709gk+0.354(1−gk)[1−(1−wk)(1−ek)(1−q)]

If the trainee's outfit has the target as a gold, sk=0.709; as a white, the bracket is 1. Each uma uses the owned outfit that gives the career the best value.

5. Career and loop. Scenario-guaranteed golds (ngold of them) each spark 0.709 of the time; the career's value is the product over the open targets, and the loop's value the mean of its 12 careers:

V=0.709ngold∏kskL=112∑c=112Vc

The product treats targets as independent; step 11 corrects it.

Deck. 6. A deck's hint odds. Six cards at max limit break; group cards count as pals. Per target k, every source j in the deck gives a gold chance gkj and a chance of any hint hkj; sources are independent:

gk=1−∏j(1−gkj), wk=1−∏j(1−hkj)1−gk

  • Training hints. h=base×(1+hint frequency/100) draws per career, read as ⌊h⌋ or ⌊h⌋+1 draws with the fraction as the weight of the second. Base: Unity Cup 2.70, Grand Concert 2.03, Trackblazer 1.81, URA Finale 1.60, Grand Masters 2.30 (placeholder). Each draw picks uniformly among the card's n hintable skills still below hint level 5; a skill leaves the pool after ν=⌈5/levels per hint⌉ draws. With k of the n skills being targets and t draws:

    P(no target hinted)=(1−kn)t, t≤ν

    (beyond ν draws the shrinking pool is followed exactly, state by state). A card's draws over several targets are one joint distribution.

  • Story chains. A card's chain link l fires with chance ml (step 7); links fire in order, so with xl the chance link l's reward gives the target, a skill offered at several links counts from the earliest that fires:

    P(no hint from the card)=1−∑lml[∏l′<l(1−xl′)−∏l′≤l(1−xl′)]
  • Fixed events (pal events and other events that don't depend on the chain) give their chance directly.
  • Event outcomes. An event gives one outcome per firing, so a gold and a white of the same skill from one event are combined within the event before the sources are multiplied. Outcomes are 50/50 unless measured (Sasami Anshinzawa SSR's finale, Riko Kashimoto SSR's Ramp Up, Seiun Sky [Foolproof Plan]'s Fast-Paced, Smart Falcon [My Umadol Way! ☆]'s Center Stage: 75%). Where an event offers targets on different choices, the card is offered once per choice worth taking (a choice no other beats on every target) and marked "prioritise X". A pal entry names the event and choice it relies on; the skill to prioritise is a skill on that choice and on no choice above it.
  • Pals. Pal finales always fire; first-year date events are left out.

7. Chain progress. The number of chain events N in a career is N∼Binomial(21,0.35), a model chosen from data gathered from shared Independent Training runs (how far each card's chain got, read from the event hints on each run's page):

P(N=e)=(21e)0.35e0.6521−e

Each event advances one chain entry i (a non-pal card with Li links, di done), picked with weight equal to its links left; an entry that reached its stop is out, and events left over when every entry has stopped are lost:

P(entry i)=Li−di∑j(Lj−dj)

A card played to an early end (a choice that ends its chain) is an entry that stops at that link, its remaining links freed for the others. If IT's default choice ends a chain on a share of its outcomes, the entry stops there with that chance, as an exact mixture over where it stops. ml is the chance entry i's link l fires, computed exactly over N and the picks; pals and R cards take no chain events.

8. One deck for the loop, or one per uma. With a shared deck, one deck D from the legal decks 𝒟 serves all 12 careers. With a deck per uma (the default), each uma u uses her own best legal deck from 𝒟u, the same in the 3 careers she heads:

Lshared=maxD∈𝒟112∑cVc(D)Lper uma=112∑umaxD∈𝒟u∑c:Tc=uVc(D)

No trainee uses a card of herself or a group card she belongs to; scenario bans apply; one card may be borrowed. The result shows the deck most umas use, then each other uma's swaps against it. "Results to show" returns up to three: with a shared deck, three distinct decks, each with its best loop; with a deck per uma, the three best loops.

9. Race schedules. Each uma's schedule is chosen to maximise the loop's expected shared G1 wins ∑i<jw(i,j), every pair weighted alike. Win chance of one attempt, with aptitude cost κ (A 0, B 10, C 20, D 30, E 40, F 60, G 90) and streak penalty σn for the n-th race in a row (0/0/5/20/30/50; any race counts, a rest resets it):

p=clamp(110−κdist−κsurf−σn,0,100)100

A race run in Classic and Senior counts once: P(win)=1−(1−p1)(1−p2); two umas' wins are independent: w(i,j)=∑rPi(r)Pj(r). One uma's best schedule against fixed partners is exact dynamic programming (Junior turns in order, then each Classic turn with the Senior turn 24 later, tracking both streaks and which career goals are met). The four schedules are improved in turns until none improves, then by group moves (two or more umas forced onto one race together), then by 30 rounds of randomised restarts; a last pass adds every optional G1 that costs no shared wins.

Search. 10. Pruning and the stop rule.

  • Decks are compared per open target on (g,1−(1−g)(1−w)), which every career's value rises with. A deck that another beats or matches on every target is dropped (the skyline); a card that another legal card beats on every target never enters those decks. With a deck per uma, each uma's best deck is on the skyline of decks for the targets her outfits leave open.

  • Every foursome F gets a ceiling. Exact pairwise bounds on w(i,j) (each uma's best schedule against a partner at her best case on every race) give each career's highest possible qcmax; Bu(q) is uma u's best career value over the skyline at inheritance chance q, which rises with q:

    ceiling(F)=112∑cBTc(qcmax)

    Foursomes are solved best ceiling first (schedules, then decks), and the search stops when no remaining ceiling can beat the last result shown. That is a guarantee relative to the schedules the optimiser finds, which are not proven optimal.

  • Character rule with one shared deck: the search first ignores who can use which card; when an answer breaks the rule it splits into loops without that uma and loops whose decks avoid the blocking cards, and searches both.

11. Exact scoring. The search ranks by step 5's product. Chain cards share one career's chain events, so their rewards land or miss together, and one event's outcomes can give one target or another. The results shown, and every candidate whose ranking value is at least 80% of the best's, are rescored exactly: over the joint distribution of chain progress (every entry's links done), given which every source is independent, except outcome groups touching two or more targets, which are enumerated. The odds shown are these exact values: 0–8% above the product on the reference setups, up to 19% for decks with 0–1 pals. "7 of 8 or better" comes from the same distribution.

Typical time: under a second with Unity Cup's golds given, 3–11 s without; up to about 100 s for three results. The browser is about 1.5× slower.
