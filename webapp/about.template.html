How this works

The planner searches loops of three or four umas, outfits, race schedules and support decks from what you own, and ranks them by the chance that one career ends with every target skill as a spark on the veteran. Independent Training only.

How the trainee acquires a skill

  • Scenario. E.g. Unity Cup gives every trainee the No Stopping Me! and It's On! golds.
  • Outfit. Skills on the trainee's own skill list, e.g. Agnes Digital [Full-Color Fangirling] has Superstan.
  • Support deck. Training hints and card events.
  • Inheritance. Two inspirations per career from the parents and grandparents.

Assumptions

  1. 8/8 legacies: both parents are 8/8, and so are all four grandparents: each carries every target spark, as in a running loop. A loop still being built has fewer carriers and lower odds.
  2. Spark generation: P(spark)=b×1.1n, with b = 20% for a white or 40% for a gold, and n the number of the six ancestors already carrying that spark. In a running loop n = 6: 35.4% and 70.9%.
  3. Assumed lineage rank: legacy sparks have 50/45/5% for 1/2/3 stars below SS, 20/70/10% at SS. The rank is chosen in the settings; the planner doesn't work out the score a deck would reach.
  4. Every card at max limit break.
  5. IT's event choices: IT takes the top choice unless a prioritised skill sits only on another choice, and priorities are set to reach the hints counted.
  6. Pal events: pal finales always fire; pal first-year date events don't fire in IT.

Limitations

  1. Only skills are scored: the planner doesn't calculate a career's rank, score or skill points.
  2. The loop planner's race scheduler does not account for pink (aptitude) sparks from legacies, so optimal loops may vary depending on which pink sparks are available on chosen parents. The career planner does count them: your veterans' pinks raise the trainee's aptitudes at the start and may fire at the April inspirations.
  3. The exact choices IT takes at events are still unclear.
  4. Search: results are ranked by exact odds. To save time, a deck is scored exactly only if its odds with targets treated as independent come within 20% of the best result; this assumes linked targets never raise a deck's odds by more than 25% (the most seen is 17%). When a search runs long the margin shrinks, and the status line says so. Race schedules are the optimiser's best found, not proven best.
  5. The counted hints rely on the player's IT priorities; the deck card names the one the planner knows (Sasami Anshinzawa SSR: prioritise Risky Business).
  6. Not modelled: random character events from support cards (about one career in six; under 2% on the odds); conditional and secret events, except Tokai Teio's Head-On; hint levels and skill-point costs; Grand Concert's low rate for the first choice of Sasami Anshinzawa SSR's finale.
  7. Placeholders: Grand Masters' hint base (2.30) is a guess, with no IT runs to set it. R cards are left out (R pals stay): their hint and event model is untested. The Grand Masters-era data is the JP game data cut at L'Arc's launch.
  8. Data: card events come from cached GameTora files and the game data from one Global master.mdb snapshot.
  9. Every measured rate comes from uma-it uploads.

The loop

Four umas take turns as the trainee. Her parents are two of the other three; her grandparents are their parents from their own last careers. No uma is her own ancestor. In a running loop all six ancestors carry every target spark. The odds shown are the average over the 12 trainee and parent arrangements.

Three umas can also rotate: each trains from the other two, who were bred from each other and from her own earlier veteran. She is then her own grandparent twice; that grandparent has no relation points with her, only the G1s it shares with its child. A rotation's odds are the average of its 3 careers, so both kinds compare on the same footing: the chance per career when every member trains in turn. A rotation usually wins: its careers are a little worse than a loop's best ones (a fourth uma as grandparent adds relation points), but a loop must also train its fourth uma, usually the weakest trainee.

Affinity and races

Affinity raises how often a legacy passes its sparks (see Spark inheritance odds). It is the umas' fixed relation points (pairs and trios) plus 3 for each G1 both won. The race schedules are chosen to maximise the loop's shared G1 wins; the Affinity button shows the totals.

Example: Oguri Cap and Agnes Digital have 30 relation points, and 21 shared G1s add 63.

Win chance of one race by the uma's distance and surface aptitude, for the first or second race in a row:

A third race in a row costs 5 more points, a fourth 20, a fifth 30, a sixth and later 50 (any race counts; a rest resets the streak). A G1 run in both Classic and Senior counts once, won if either attempt wins.

In a schedule, "A or B" means either race at the same odds; every uma of the loop must run the same one. Optional G1s add race sparks, not affinity.

The deck

  • Training hints: the game doesn't publish how often a card hints. These rates are inferred from what real shared IT runs show, not read from the game's data, so they are estimates. The rate scales with the card's hint frequency: base×(1+hint frequency/100) hints per career, each a random pick among the card's skills still below hint level 5. The base differs by scenario: Unity Cup 2.70, Grand Concert 2.03, Trackblazer 1.81, URA Finale 1.60 (Grand Masters 2.30, a placeholder). A card with 30% hint frequency hints about 3.5 times in Unity Cup; with five hintable skills, a given one comes up in about half of careers.
  • Chain events: the number of story-chain events in a career is random, also estimated from real runs: 7.35 on average, between 5 and 10 in 83% of careers. Each one advances a non-pal card's chain, picked at random, a card with more links left more often; the same in every scenario. Three 3-link cards (9 links) usually leave some last links unplayed. Pals have their own storyline and take none.
  • Event outcomes: 50/50 unless measured. Measured at 75%: Sasami Anshinzawa SSR's Nothing Ventured, Riko Kashimoto SSR's Ramp Up, Seiun Sky [Foolproof Plan]'s Fast-Paced, Smart Falcon [My Umadol Way! ☆]'s Center Stage.
  • A card marked "prioritise X" needs X set as an IT priority for its event to give the counted reward.
  • Every deck may borrow one friend's card, and does when that card makes it better. By default each uma gets her own best deck; a result shows the shared deck, then each uma's swaps.
  • No trainee can use a card of herself or a group card she belongs to. Team Sirius is banned in Unity Cup and Grand Concert.

Reading a result

  • 1 in 107 runs to land all 8: one career in 107 on average. "7 of 8 or better" counts careers that miss at most one.
  • 90% chance within 245 runs: the number of careers that gives a 90% chance of at least one success. After 107 careers, 37% of players still have none.
  • As trainee: the odds when that uma is the trainee; they differ by outfit.

Spark inheritance odds

A career has two inspirations, in April of the second and third years. At each one, every legacy (two parents and four grandparents) rolls each of its sparks separately:

P(pass)=base×(1+affinity100)
  • Base by spark type and stars (1/2/3★): skill (white) 3/6/9%, race 1/2/3%, unique (green) 5/10/15%, aptitude (pink) 1/3/5%, stat (blue) 70/80/90%.
  • Stars of a target spark: 50/45/5% below SS, an average base of 4.65%; 20/70/10% at SS, 5.7%.
  • Affinity is that legacy's own, so parents pass sparks far more often than grandparents.

A target is inherited when any of the twelve rolls (six legacies, two inspirations) passes, with Pi each roll's P(pass):

P=1−∏i=112(1−Pi)

Example: a parent at affinity 286 passes a given skill spark about 18% of the time per inspiration, a grandparent at 87 about 9%. A skill all six legacies carry is inherited in about 78% of careers.

The model, in detail

Very technical: the full model and search, written so another person or program could reimplement the planner. Nothing here is needed to use it.

Show the model

In short.

  1. For a loop of three or four umas, each uma's race schedule is chosen to maximise the G1s they win together. That sets each legacy's affinity, and so each career's chance of inheriting a target (steps 1–3, 9).
  2. A career's value is its chance of landing every target, from the trainee's outfit, the deck's hints (training, events, story chains) and inheritance. A loop's value is the average of its careers, 12 or 3 (steps 4–8).
  3. Exact odds account for targets that land together (chain cards share one career's chain events), but they are slow. A fast estimate that treats targets as independent picks which loops and decks are worth scoring exactly (steps 10–13).
  4. The search goes through loops from the most promising down, finds each trainee's best deck by exact odds, and stops when nothing left can beat the results shown. The status line says how thoroughly the answer was checked (steps 14–20).

Symbols: T the trainee, P a parent and P′ the other parent, X the fourth uma of the loop (in a rotation, the trainee herself); k a target, D a deck.

Model. 1. Careers. A loop of four umas has 12 careers: a trainee T (4 ways) and the uma X who sits out (3 ways). The other two are the parents; each parent P's own parents are P′ and X (the grandparents). Each uma heads 3 careers. A three-uma rotation has 3 careers, one per trainee T: her parents are the other two, and each parent's own parents are the other parent and T, so X=T below (her own earlier veteran, with her schedule's wins). A trio counts only distinct umas, so trio(T,P,T)=0 (fitted on 101,536 inspirations from IT runs: counting it as pair(T,P) predicts 16% more passes from such grandparents than seen).

2. Affinity of each legacy. pair and trio are the game's relation points (the sum over the relation groups all of them belong to); w(i,j) is the expected number of G1s both i and j win, from their schedules (step 9):

AP=pair(T,P)+pair(P,P′)+trio(T,P,P′)+trio(T,P,X)+3[2w(P,P′)+w(P,X)]AG=trio(T,P,G)+3w(P,G), G ∈ {P′, X}

Relation points come from the game's succession-relation table: each relation group has a point value and a member list; pair(a,b) sums the points of every group holding both, trio(a,b,c) of every group holding all three (three distinct umas). Two umas share a G1 when both win the same race: races are matched by race id, with the three relocated venues counted as their usual race. The results' affinity table shows exactly these per-legacy values.

3. Inheritance chance. Every target spark sits on all six legacies (two parents, four grandparents), with stars drawn from the rank's mix s1/s2/s3 (50/45/5% below SS, 20/70/10% at SS). The chance that one roll passes a spark averages the 3/6/9% bases over that mix; two inspirations, so the chance that a target is inherited at least once in the career is the same q for every target:

b=0.03s1+0.06s2+0.09s3q=1−(∏ℓ(1−min(1,b(1+Aℓ100))))2

4. One target's spark chance. For target k: gk the deck's chance of a gold hint, wk its chance of a white hint given no gold hint (step 6), ek the chance the trainee's own events hint it. A skill the trainee learns becomes a spark 0.709 of the time as a gold and 0.354 as a white (0.2×1.16 or 0.4×1.16):

sk=0.709gk+0.354(1−gk)[1−(1−wk)(1−ek)(1−q)]

If the trainee's outfit has the target as a gold, sk=0.709; as a white, the bracket is 1. A uma's owned outfits (minus any excluded) are grouped by what they give: per open target, 2 if the outfit's skill list has the gold, 1 if the white (also 1 for a target the scenario gives as a white), else 0, raised by skills the trainee's own story always gives; ek=1−∏(1−ch) over the trainee's own hint events h for that skill, chance ch each. Outfits with the same coverage and ek are one option. A uma with no owned outfit uses "any outfit" (no coverage). Each career uses the option with the best value (ties: the first listed).

5. Career and loop. Scenario-guaranteed golds (ngold of them) each spark 0.709 of the time. A career's value V is its chance of landing every target: exact over how the targets' hints depend on each other (step 19). Treating the open targets as independent gives the product

V^=0.709ngold∏ksk

which the search only uses to bound its work (step 10). A loop's value is the mean over its n careers (12 for four umas, 3 for a rotation), and both kinds are ranked together on it:

L=1n∑c=1nVc

Deck. 6. Deck entries. Six slots, every card at max limit break. The cards a deck can use: owned ones (or every card, when nothing is marked), plus at most one card the player doesn't own (a friend's, used only when it helps); never an excluded card or one the scenario bans (Team Sirius in Unity Cup and Grand Concert). A card enters only if it can give a target (by training, an event or a chain) or is locked into every deck. Pal and group cards, and cards without chain events (R cards), take no chain slot; chain cards are SR or SSR. A card becomes one entry per way of playing it:

  • per choice worth taking at each chain link where an event offers targets on different choices (a choice is dropped when another gives every target at least its chances of a gold and of any hint): the deck shows "link 2: Uma Stan", the skill to prioritise;
  • per early end: played to a choice that ends its chain at link ℓ, the card's links before ℓ as usual, that choice's rewards at ℓ, and its later links freed for the other cards.

All of a card's entries share its character, so a deck holds at most one, and no two cards of one character. Slots left over hold owned pal characters with no useful card (no chain slot, no rewards; those blocking fewest umas first) or, failing that, a generic SR without target rewards (a 2-link chain slot: it still takes chain events). With "pals" set, the deck has exactly that many pal and group cards, the plain pals included.

6b. A deck's hint odds. Group cards count as pals. Per target k, every source j in the deck gives a gold chance gkj and a chance of any hint hkj; sources are independent:

gk=1−∏j(1−gkj), wk=1−∏j(1−hkj)1−gk

  • Training hints. h=base×(1+hint frequency/100) draws per career, read as ⌊h⌋ or ⌊h⌋+1 draws with the fraction as the weight of the second. Base: Unity Cup 2.70, Grand Concert 2.03, Trackblazer 1.81, URA Finale 1.60, Grand Masters 2.30 (placeholder). Each draw picks uniformly among the card's n hintable skills still below hint level 5; a skill leaves the pool after ν=⌈5/levels per hint⌉ draws. With k of the n skills being targets and t draws:

    P(no target hinted)=(1−kn)t, t≤ν

    (beyond ν draws the shrinking pool is followed exactly: a state counts how many of the other skills have taken 0, 1, …, ν draws, each draw picks uniformly among skills still in the pool, and the chance is the total of states in which none of the k was drawn). Hint frequency is the card's value at max limit break plus its unique effect's; the hint levels per draw are 1 plus its hint-level bonus. Training gives whites only. A card's draws over several targets are one joint distribution: with m of its skills targets and M(j) the chance that none of j given ones is drawn, exactly the targets in S are hinted with chance

    P(S)=∑U⊆S(−1)|S|−|U|M(m−|U|)

    an event with one outcome per subset (steps 13 and 19 treat it like any event).

  • Story chains. A card's chain link l fires with chance ml (step 7); links fire in order, so with xl the chance link l's reward gives the target, a skill offered at several links counts from the earliest that fires:

    P(no hint from the card)=1−∑lml[∏l′<l(1−xl′)−∏l′≤l(1−xl′)]
  • Fixed events (pal events and other events that don't depend on the chain) give their chance directly.
  • Event outcomes. Events are decoded from GameTora's event data: an event belongs to the chain link the game shows for it; a choice's rewards split into outcomes at each "or"; an outcome that ends the chain marks the choice as an early end (all outcomes) or, when it is IT's default choice and only some outcomes end it, a stop risk (the chain stops there with chance k/n, step 7). An event gives one outcome per firing, so a gold and a white of the same skill from one event are combined within the event before the sources are multiplied (within one outcome the gold counts), and two rewards at one link from separate rows multiply as independent. Outcomes are 50/50 unless measured (Sasami Anshinzawa SSR's finale, Riko Kashimoto SSR's Ramp Up, Seiun Sky [Foolproof Plan]'s Fast-Paced, Smart Falcon [My Umadol Way! ☆]'s Center Stage: 75%). Where an event offers targets on different choices, the card is offered once per choice worth taking (a choice no other beats on every target) and marked "prioritise X". A pal entry names the event and choice it relies on; the skill to prioritise is a skill on that choice and on no choice above it.
  • Pals. Pal finales always fire; first-year date events are left out.
  • Deck totals. With Gj source j's chance of no gold and Hj of no hint: gk=1−∏Gj, and the chance of any hint is 1−∏Hj; the deck output gives the gold chance and the white chance given no gold.

7. Chain progress. The number of chain events N in a career is N∼Binomial(21,0.35), a model chosen from data gathered from shared Independent Training runs (how far each card's chain got, read from the event hints on each run's page):

P(N=e)=(21e)0.35e0.6521−e

Each event advances one chain entry i (a non-pal card with Li links, di done), picked with weight equal to its links left; an entry that reached its stop is out, and events left over when every entry has stopped are lost:

P(entry i)=Li−di∑j(Lj−dj)

A card played to an early end (a choice that ends its chain) is an entry that stops at that link, its remaining links freed for the others. If IT's default choice ends a chain on a share of its outcomes, the entry stops there with that chance, as an exact mixture over where it stops. ml is the chance entry i's link l fires, computed exactly over N and the picks; pals and R cards take no chain events.

8. One deck for the loop, or one per uma. With a shared deck, one deck D from the legal decks 𝒟 serves all the loop's careers. With a deck per uma (the default), each uma u uses her own best legal deck from 𝒟u, the same in the careers she heads (3 in a four-uma loop, 1 in a rotation):

Lshared=maxD∈𝒟1n∑cVc(D)Lper uma=1n∑umaxD∈𝒟u∑c:Tc=uVc(D)

Vc(D) is career c's exact value with deck D. No trainee uses a card of herself or a group card she belongs to; scenario bans apply; one card may be borrowed. The result shows the deck most umas use, then each other uma's swaps against it. "Results to show" returns up to three: with a shared deck, three distinct decks, each with its best loop; with a deck per uma, the three best loops. Two decks are the same when they hold the same cards, played the same way (choices, early ends), with the same number of plain pals.

9. Race schedules. A career has 72 turns (Junior 1–24, Classic 25–48, Senior 49–72). A uma's options on a turn: rest, every G1 run that turn, and her career-goal races (a goal race replaces a G1 it coincides with); at most five races per turn. A turn her career's story forbids racing offers only rest or her goal race there (Yamanin Zephyr can't run Classic late October, so no Tenno Sho (Autumn), Shuka Sho or Kikuka Sho that year; Sweep Tosho can't run the Classic Takarazuka Kinen). Goals: a goal tied to one turn forces one of its races there; a goal spanning turns needs one of its (turn, race) picks. Aptitudes are those of the uma's first outfit; distance by length: up to 1400 m sprint, 1800 m mile, 2400 m medium, else long. Each uma's schedule is chosen to maximise the loop's expected shared G1 wins ∑i<jw(i,j), every pair weighted alike: summed over a structure's careers, step 2 counts each pair's w 20 times in a four-uma loop and 10 times in a rotation (per career 6 times for the parents' pair, twice for each parent with X), the same for every pair in both. Win chance of one attempt, with aptitude cost κ (A 0, B 10, C 20, D 30, E 40, F 60, G 90) and streak penalty σn for the n-th race in a row (0/0/5/20/30/50; any race counts, a rest resets it):

p=clamp(110−κdist−κsurf−σn,0,100)100

A race run in Classic and Senior counts once: P(win)=1−(1−p1)(1−p2); two umas' wins are independent: w(i,j)=∑rPi(r)Pj(r). One uma's best schedule against fixed partners maximises ∑rWrPi(r), with Wr the partners' summed chances of winning race r, by exact dynamic programming: Junior turns in order with state (streak, goals met); then each Classic turn paired with the Senior turn 24 later, tracking both streaks, the goals met and a guessed Classic streak at the end of Classic, which the Senior streak starts from and the end state must match. Ties go to resting, then to the earlier-listed race. The search over the loop's schedules:

  1. Start: each uma's best schedule against her partners' best cases (every race they could run, at streak 1).
  2. Ascent: each uma in turn re-solves against the others' current schedules; repeat until none improves by more than 10−9.
  3. Group moves: for each turn, and each target there (rest, or a race), each subset of two or more umas who could move to it is forced there one after another, then ascent runs; a move is kept if the total rises by more than 10−7 (a forced state more than 60 below the current total is dropped before the ascent). Repeat until none is kept.
  4. 30 restarts from the best found: one or two umas (at random) re-solve against race weights each multiplied by a random factor in [0.2, 1.8], then ascent and group moves; kept if better by more than 10−7. The random numbers are a fixed-seed xorshift generator, so a job gives the same schedules every run.
  5. Fill: each uma re-solves with every race weight raised by 10−7 (among schedules worth the same, the one running more G1s), kept if the total doesn't drop; then every rest turn that isn't forced gets any G1 (highest win chance first) that keeps the goals met, costs no other race any win chance and leaves the total unchanged.

Pairs' and trios' relation points don't depend on the schedules, so this maximises the six pairs' shared wins ∑i<jw(i,j) equally weighted. Schedules are chosen for affinity, not for the skill odds. The search is a heuristic: not proven optimal. Pairwise bound, for any schedules (used by the search): w(a,b)≤min(BRa(Pbmax),BRb(Pamax)), where Pbmax counts every race b could run at streak 1 (no schedule wins it more) and BRa is a's exact best response to those weights.

Search. 10. Exact ranking and the cap. Write V(u,q,D) for the exact value of a career with trainee u, deck D and inheritance chance q (step 19), and V^(u,q,D) for step 5's product. Loops and decks are ranked by V. V^ is cheap and rises with every deck odd (for 0≤q≤1, a white being worth less than a gold), so it bounds groups of decks and loops at once; it only decides what gets scored, under one assumption:

V≤ρV^,ρ=1.25

The largest V/V^ seen on any deck scored is 1.17 (Grand Masters, decks with 0–1 pals); on Global jobs, 1.04. Every deck scored is checked against it, and the largest ratio seen is reported with the result.

Why not rank by exact odds alone: on the Global default (Unity Cup, everything owned), one trainee has 15.1 million legal decks, even with interchangeable cards merged. Listing them takes 3–4 s and scoring each exactly 21–25 µs, so the best deck for one trainee at one inheritance chance takes about 6 minutes natively. The search needs that value for every uma it considers (68 in that pool) at several chances: hours to days per search. The exact value of a half-built deck says nothing about its completions; V^ of a half-built deck bounds every completion (it rises with every deck odd), so whole branches of millions of decks are cut at once. The proven bound U below costs about 3 µs a deck (45 s for all 15.1 million) and cannot bound a half-built deck either.

11. The product skyline. Decks are compared on two numbers per open target, g and 1−(1−g)(1−w) (only those some card can move). The skyline is the decks no other deck matches or beats on every one; V^'s maximum over all decks, for any trainee and chance, is reached on it. It is built one combination of chain kinds at a time (step 12), which fixes every card's chain odds, with two exact shortcuts:

  • Card dominance. A card is left out when at least six cards of other characters and the same chain kind (owned whenever it is) beat it on every target even at their weakest (chain odds of the most crowded deck) against it at its strongest (every link firing): any deck holding it has a swap at least as good.
  • Swap rule. Under a combination of chain kinds, a deck holding a card that a card outside it beats on every target, and that could legally replace it (same kind, a character not already in the deck, within the borrow limit), is skipped: the swapped deck is generated too and is at least as good. A half-built deck that already has such a swap, whatever is added, is cut.

For each uma the skyline is thinned to the numbers her outfits leave open (both where some outfit lacks the skill, g alone where they have at most the white). Product tables B^u(q) give her best V^ over that view at each grid chance (step 15), found through clusters of decks with their per-coordinate maxima, best bound first. A trainee's product ceiling in a loop is her best single skyline deck over her careers; with a shared deck, the best single skyline deck over all the loop's careers. These bound V^ of every deck, legal or not.

12. Every legal deck. The exact search draws from every legal deck, not the skyline. For one trainee (or all the loop's members with a shared deck) the entries are those no member's character rule excludes; entries interchangeable for those trainees are merged (the same rewards on every target some outfit of theirs leaves open, or none: of the latter, at most six per chain kind are kept, characters no rewarding card uses first), so every deck has an equal-valued deck among those enumerated. A deck is a configuration and a choice within it:

  • Configuration: how many entries of each chain kind (links, stop link, stop risk), with generic 2-link fillers counted among the 2-link slots; how many entries without chain slots; plain pals for the remaining slots. Configurations whose groups cannot hold every locked card, or that miss a fixed pal count, are skipped.
  • Within a configuration, entries are picked group by group in a fixed order: one per character, at most one borrowed card, every locked card placed (a branch that can no longer place one is cut).

Under a configuration every entry's chain odds are fixed, so each entry has per-target miss chances. A half-built deck, with r picks left in some groups, is dropped when the best value any completion could reach, times ρ, is below the threshold. Per (trainee, chance, outfit), two bounds, the lower used:

  • each target at the best miss chances the remaining picks could give it: per group, the product of the r lowest;
  • the half-built deck's product times the r best ratios rj=V^({j})/V^(∅) of each group's cards. Valid because logV^ of one outfit at one chance is submodular in the set of cards: each target's factor is a−bmg(1+cmw) in the deck's miss products, so a card gains less the more the deck already holds.

Configurations are visited best root bound first; the search stops at the first one whose bound fails. At most 50,000 decks per search; more ends the pass (step 18).

13. A proven filter. Before a deck is scored exactly, it is bounded by U≥V. Let x be the vector of links done after N=n chain events. For every combination of chain kinds and every n, it is checked exactly, over the distribution of step 7 (early ends and stop risk included), that x is negatively upper-orthant dependent:

P(x≥t∣n)≤∏iP(xi≥ti∣n)for every threshold vectort

Each card's chain is grouped with the targets it feeds; group c's factor Φc (the product of its targets' sk, other sources included) rises with that card's progress alone, and is a positive combination of indicators {xi≥t}, so

V≤U=0.709ngoldmaxoutfit∑nP(N=n)∏cE[Φc∣n]∏knot fed by a chainsk

Events and training draws enter at each target's marginal chance. That is an upper bound only when, for each such group touching two or more targets, the targets' hint indicators are negatively upper-orthant dependent too (for every set S of its targets, P(all ofS)≤∏k∈SP(k)): true for outcomes that each give one target, which exclude each other; checked exactly for every group, and a deck with a group that fails skips the filter. A target fed by two cards' chains puts both cards in one group; the progress check is then made over every up-set of that pair (any rising function of two coordinates is a positive combination of up-set indicators). Decks with three cards feeding one target, or two such pairs, skip the filter. U is within 0.2% of V on the reference setups and filters out almost every candidate.

14. One trainee's exact best deck. For trainee u at her chances q1,… (her three careers, or one grid chance) under cap ρ:

  1. score her three best legal skyline decks by V^ exactly (or, if none is legal, the legal deck with the best V^);
  2. list every legal deck (step 12) whose ρV^ reaches the best exact value so far;
  3. in order of V^, best first, until ρV^ falls below the best exact value: skip the deck if U does, else score it exactly.

The result is cached per (trainee, chances) with the cap it holds under; a higher cap extends it.

15. Exact ceilings. Bu(q)=maxD∈𝒟uV(u,q,D) on 64 chances evenly spaced from the lowest possible q (every affinity 0) to the highest any trainee can reach (each uma's highest: her best parents, with the pairwise bounds of step 9 for shared wins and the best possible fourth member, or the trainee herself, in every slot it fills). V rises with q, so with each career's chance rounded up to the grid a loop's ceiling is

ceiling(F)=1n∑cBTc(qc)

Each career may take its own deck here, so this bounds the shared and per-uma values alike. A cell is computed (one step 14 search at one chance) only when a ceiling that could still win needs it; until then the lowest computed cell at a higher grid point stands in, or ρ times B^u. While a ceiling beats the result to beat, its loosest term is computed: first at that uma's highest grid point, then halfway down towards the one needed, and so on.

16. Loops. With Mu uma u's best product value at her highest chance, a loop's value is at most the mean of its members' Mu times the guaranteed golds' factor (each uma heads the same number of careers). Loops of both sizes are generated in bands of that mean: first down to 85% of the largest sum, then each next band down to where it can no longer beat the result to beat divided by ρ, but at most 15% below the previous band's bottom at a time. Each generated loop gets a product ceiling from the tables at the chances the pairwise bounds allow. Then, best first, a loop:

  1. is dropped if the ceiling from cells already computed can't beat the result to beat, or ρ times its product ceiling (step 11) can't;
  2. is dropped if its exact ceiling (step 15) at the chances the pairwise bounds allow can't, refining cells as needed;
  3. has its schedules optimised (step 9; cached across passes), and is dropped if its exact ceiling at its schedules' chances can't (counted as rejected after scheduling: a better schedule could change its fate);
  4. gets its exact value: each member's exact best deck over her careers (step 14).

The search stops when no remaining loop's product ceiling times ρ, and no ungenerated band's bound times ρ, can beat the last result shown (the third with three results). Ties keep the earlier-solved loop.

17. One shared deck. A loop's value is the best exact sum over its careers among decks legal for all its members (step 14 over them at once, scored as the sum of their careers, seeded with the best k+2 legal skyline decks for k results). Its ceiling is the lower of step 15's and ρ times the best single skyline deck's product over its careers. With three results, each a distinct deck with its best loop, each solved loop keeps every deck at or above the third-best distinct deck found so far (on the first loop, its own three best), so a deck's best loop is always among those kept.

18. What the answer is checked against. The search at ρ=1.25 runs within a fixed amount of work, 30 million units of about 0.1 µs each, counted in steps of the search, not seconds, so the answer does not depend on the machine: 2 per half-built deck tested, 5 per deck listed, 10,000 per deck search, 25 per filter bound (100 with a shared deck), 200 per exact score (800 with a shared deck). If it runs out, or a deck search exceeds 50,000 decks, the search reruns at ρ = 1.12, then 1.05, each with a quarter of the budget more and reusing every exact value, table cell and schedule already computed (cells from a cut-short deck search are discarded). If none finishes, loops are ranked by V^ and each shown result is scored exactly. The status line says which cap the result was checked under. The page runs the product ranking first and shows it at once, then the exact search. Either way the guarantee is relative to the schedules found (step 9), which are not proven optimal.

19. Exact scoring. Chain cards share one career's chain events, so their rewards land or miss together, and one event's outcomes can give one target or another. Let x be the final chain progress (links done per card) with probability π(x) from step 7: P(N=ℓ) times the chance of reaching x, where ℓ is the number of links done in x, or P(N≥ℓ) when every card has stopped (a stop risk is a mixture over where the card stops). Given x, every source is independent, except events whose outcomes touch two or more targets (a card's training draws over several targets included); let O be those events' outcomes. Then

V=0.709ngoldmaxoutfit∑xπ(x)∑OP(O)∏ksk(x,O)

with sk(x,O) step 4's formula, its hint odds from the sources given x (each card's rewards from links up to its progress) and O (a target an outcome gives is hinted for sure, as a gold if the outcome gives the gold). Computed as: progress values that change no target's odds are merged (filler slots drop out), each remaining state's per-target miss products are built from per-card tables by links done, and the outcome events are enumerated per cluster of targets they join (clusters are independent given x). The outfit is chosen per career by the exact value. "7 of 8 or better" is the same sum with the chance that at most one target misses (guaranteed golds counted as targets that miss with chance 0.291), from each independent unit's chance that all its targets land and that exactly one misses.

20. What is reported. Per result: the loop's exact odds (the mean of its careers'), "7 of 8 or better", each trainee's mean over her careers, the mean inheritance chance, the schedules, each lineage's affinities, and the deck or decks. A trainee's outfit shown is the option used in most of her careers. With a deck per uma, the deck most members use comes first and each other member's swaps against it. The status line gives the cap the result was checked under; the solver also reports the product value of the result, the largest exact/product ratio seen on any deck scored, and the counts of loops solved and decks scored.

Typical time: the product-ranked result shows in well under a second, the exact one usually 2–5 s later; up to about 35 s for three results with one shared deck.
