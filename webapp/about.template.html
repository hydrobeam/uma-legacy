How this works

The planner searches four-uma loops, outfits, race schedules and support decks from what you own, and ranks them by the chance that one career ends with every target skill as a spark on the veteran. Independent Training only.

How the trainee acquires a skill

  • Scenario. Unity Cup gives every trainee the No Stopping Me! and It's On! golds.
  • Outfit. Skills on the trainee's own skill list: Agnes Digital [Full-Color Fangirling] has Superstan; Tamamo Cross [Fast as Lightning] and Inari One [Edomurasaki] have Tail Nine. Yamanin Zephyr gets Slipstream from a goal race.
  • Support deck. Training hints and card events.
  • Inheritance. Two inspirations per career from the parents and grandparents.

Assumptions

  1. 8/8 legacies: both parents are 8/8, and so are all four grandparents: each carries every target spark, as in a running loop. A loop still being built has fewer carriers and lower odds.
  2. Spark generation: P(spark) = b × 1.1n, with b = 20% for a white or 40% for a gold, and n the number of the six ancestors already carrying that spark. In a running loop n = 6: 35.4% and 70.9%.
  3. Star mix of legacy sparks: 50/45/5% for 1/2/3 stars below SS; 20/70/10% at SS.
  4. Every card at max limit break.
  5. IT's event choices: IT takes the top choice unless a prioritised skill sits only on another choice, and priorities are set to reach the hints counted.
  6. Pal events: pal finales always fire; pal first-year date events don't fire in IT.

Limitations

  1. The exact choices IT takes at events are still unclear.
  2. The counted hints rely on the player's IT priorities; the deck card names the one the planner knows (Sasami Anshinzawa SSR: prioritise Risky Business).
  3. Not modelled: random character events from support cards (about one career in six; under 2% on the odds); conditional and secret events, except Tokai Teio's Head-On; hint levels and skill-point costs; Grand Concert's low rate for the first choice of Sasami Anshinzawa SSR's finale.
  4. Placeholders: Grand Masters' hint base (2.30) is a guess, with no IT runs to set it. R cards are off by default: their hint and event model is untested. The Grand Masters-era data is the JP game data cut at L'Arc's launch.
  5. Data: card events come from cached GameTora files and the game data from one Global master.mdb snapshot.
  6. Every measured rate comes from uma-it uploads.

The loop

Four umas take turns as the trainee. Her parents are two of the other three; her grandparents are their parents from their own last careers. No uma is her own ancestor. In a running loop all six ancestors carry every target spark. The odds shown are the average over the 12 trainee and parent arrangements.

Affinity and races

Affinity raises how often a legacy passes its sparks (see Spark inheritance odds). It is the umas' fixed relation points (pairs and trios) plus 3 for each G1 both won. The race schedules are chosen to maximise the loop's shared G1 wins; the Affinity button shows the totals.

Example: Oguri Cap and Agnes Digital have 30 relation points, and 21 shared G1s add 63.

In a schedule, "A or B" means either race at the same odds; all four umas must run the same one. Optional G1s add race sparks, not affinity.

The deck

  • Training hints: the game doesn't publish how often a card hints. These rates are inferred from what real shared IT runs show, not read from the game's data, so they are estimates. The rate scales with the card's hint frequency: base × (1 + hint frequency / 100) hints per career, each a random pick among the card's skills still below hint level 5. The base differs by scenario: Unity Cup 2.70, Grand Concert 2.03, Trackblazer 1.81, URA Finale 1.60 (Grand Masters 2.30, a placeholder). A card with 30% hint frequency hints about 3.5 times in Unity Cup; with five hintable skills, a given one comes up in about half of careers.
  • Chain events: the number of story-chain events in a career is random, also estimated from real runs: 7.35 on average, between 5 and 10 in 83% of careers. Each one advances a non-pal card's chain, picked at random, a card with more links left more often; the same in every scenario. Three 3-link cards (9 links) usually leave some last links unplayed. Pals have their own storyline and take none.
  • Event outcomes: 50/50 unless measured. Measured at 75%: Sasami Anshinzawa SSR's Nothing Ventured, Riko Kashimoto SSR's Ramp Up, Seiun Sky [Foolproof Plan]'s Fast-Paced, Smart Falcon [My Umadol Way! ☆]'s Center Stage.
  • A card marked "prioritise X" needs X set as an IT priority for its event to give the counted reward.
  • One friend's card can be borrowed. By default each uma gets her own best deck; a result shows the shared deck, then each uma's swaps.
  • No trainee can use a card of herself or a group card she belongs to. Team Sirius is banned in Unity Cup and Grand Concert.

Reading a result

  • 1 in 119 runs to land all 8: one career in 119 on average. "7 of 8 or better" counts careers that miss at most one.
  • 90% chance within 273 runs: the number of careers that gives a 90% chance of at least one success. After 119 careers, 37% of players still have none.
  • As trainee: the odds when that uma is the trainee; they differ by outfit.

Spark inheritance odds

A career has two inspirations, in April of the second and third years. At each one, every legacy (two parents and four grandparents) rolls each of its sparks separately:

P(pass) = base × (1 + affinity / 100)

  • Base by spark type and stars (1/2/3★): skill (white) 3/6/9%, race 1/2/3%, unique (green) 5/10/15%, aptitude (pink) 1/3/5%, stat (blue) 70/80/90%.
  • Stars of a target spark: 50/45/5% below SS, an average base of 4.65%; 20/70/10% at SS, 5.7%.
  • Affinity is that legacy's own, so parents pass sparks far more often than grandparents.

A target is inherited when any of the twelve rolls (six legacies, two inspirations) passes: P = 1 − Π (1 − P(pass)).

Example: a parent at affinity 286 passes a given skill spark about 18% of the time per inspiration, a grandparent at 87 about 9%. A skill all six legacies carry is inherited in about 78% of careers.

The model, in detail

Model. Each target spark sits on all six legacies with stars from the chosen rank: 50/45/5% for 1/2/3 stars below SS and 20/70/10% at SS (room-match data); a spark's base rate is 3/6/9% by stars. A skill is acquired if guaranteed, on the trainee's own outfit skill list (fully awakened), hinted by the deck, or inherited: proc = 1 − Π(1 − base × (1 + affinity/100)) over the six legacies, two inspirations. Each legacy's affinity follows uma-it-optimizer's fit to 8,155 Independent Training receipts: a parent counts its relation points with the trainee, the full relation with the other parent, its trios with the trainee and its own parents, and 3 per G1 shared with the other parent and with each of its own parents; a grandparent counts its trio with the trainee and its child, plus 3 per G1 shared with that child. It then becomes a spark 35.4% of the time as a white, 70.9% as a gold: 20% and 40%, ×1.1 for each of the six ancestors already carrying it (fit to room-match data). Odds average the 12 runs of the self-copy-free cycle.

Deck. Six cards, every card at max limit break; group cards count as pals. Training hints: base × (1 + hint frequency/100) draws per run (unique effect included), each a uniform draw over the card's skills still below hint Lv 5 (a skill at Lv 5 leaves the pool, so a card that gives +5 levels per hint never repeats one); the base depends on the scenario (Unity Cup 2.70, Grand Concert 2.03, URA Finale 1.60, Trackblazer 1.81, and Grand Masters, JP Grand Masters-era data only, 2.30 as a placeholder: no runs to measure), and one card's draws cannot hint every target in its pool at once. Chain events: a Binomial(21, 0.35) number per run (mean 7.35, the same in every scenario), each advancing a random remaining chain slot of a non-pal card, a card with more links left more often. Both are fitted to Independent Training runs shared on uma-it (training hints from 62,870 card observations in 2,402 runs, chain progress from 1,792 runs' event hints). A chain whose default choice can end it stops there at that outcome's share. Event rewards: GameTora training-event data; a reward on some of a choice's outcomes counts at its share of them (one of two: 50%) unless the share was measured on uma-it runs (Riko Kashimoto SSR's Ramp Up, Seiun Sky [Foolproof Plan]'s Fast-Paced and Smart Falcon [My Umadol Way! ☆]'s Center Stage: 75%), and an event's outcomes exclude each other. Where an event offers targets on different choices, the deck takes one (the skill to prioritise is shown on the card). Pal finales always fire: Light Hello SSR's gives See Ya Later!; Anshinzawa SSR's gives Nothing Ventured 75% of the time with Risky Business prioritised and Risky Business otherwise (measured on 77 uma-it runs with Risky Business prioritised, not the even split the rule above would give); first-year date events are left out (they don't fire in independent training). The deck and the loop are searched together, ranked by the product of each target's odds; the decks and loops near the top are then scored exactly, so the odds shown include what targets share (chain cards draw on one run's chain events, so their rewards land or miss together: on the reference setups exact odds were 0–8% higher than the product, up to 19% for decks with 0–1 pals).

Search. Every foursome gets a guaranteed ceiling from exact pairwise bounds; loops are scheduled best-first until no remaining ceiling can beat the 5th result. Schedules maximise the expected shared G1 wins, every pair weighted alike (each pair carries the same weight across the 12 careers). Win odds per race: 110 − distance cost − surface cost (A 0, B 10, C 20, D 30, E 40, F 60, G 90), minus 0/0/5/20/30/50 for the 1st to 6th+ race in a row, capped at 0–100%. Data: Global master.mdb, relocated venues count as the same race. The Grand Masters-era option uses a JP master.mdb cut at L'Arc's launch (24 Aug 2023).
